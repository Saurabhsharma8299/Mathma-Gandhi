Mahatma Gandhi: An Introduction

Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi (2 October 1869 – 30 January 1948) was a prominent Indian political and spiritual leader. He fought for India's independence through non-violent civil disobedience (Satyagraha).

Gandhi led several important Satyagraha movements that influenced not only India but the entire world. He is known as the Father of the Nation and Bapu.

Gandhi's birthday, 2nd October, is celebrated in India as Gandhi Jayanti, a national holiday, and worldwide as the International Day of Nonviolence.

What is Satyagraha?

Satyagraha is a compound word made up of 'Satya' (truth) and 'Agraha' (insistence). According to Gandhi, Satyagraha means 'insistence on truth' or 'holding onto truth'.

Satyagraha is a method of non-violent resistance where injustice is fought through peaceful means. It is the victory of love, tolerance, and truth over violence, anger, and hatred.

Major Satyagrahas by Mahatma Gandhi

1917

Champaran Satyagraha

Gandhi's first Satyagraha in India. Farmers in Champaran were being forced to cultivate indigo.

  • Took place in Champaran district, Bihar
  • Against exploitation of farmers
  • Gandhi's first major success in India
  • British had to accept farmers' demands
1918

Kheda Satyagraha

Protest against tax collection during famine in Kheda district, Gujarat.

  • In Kheda district, Gujarat
  • For tax relief during famine
  • Supported by Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel
  • Farmers got tax relief
1918

Ahmedabad Mill Strike

Strike by textile mill workers in Ahmedabad for plague bonus increase.

  • For textile mill workers
  • Demand for increased plague bonus
  • First use of hunger strike by Gandhi
  • Workers' demands were accepted
1920-1922

Non-Cooperation Movement

Movement started in protest against Jallianwala Bagh massacre and Rowlatt Act.

  • Boycott of British institutions
  • Burning of foreign clothes
  • Boycott of educational institutions
  • Withdrawn after Chauri Chaura incident
1930

Salt Satyagraha (Dandi March)

240-mile march from Ahmedabad to Dandi to protest against salt tax.

  • To break salt laws
  • 240-mile foot march
  • Started with 78 satyagrahis
  • Movement spread across the country
1906-1914

South Africa Satyagraha

Gandhi's first Satyagraha for rights of Indians in South Africa.

  • Against Asiatic Registration Act
  • For civil rights of Indians
  • First use of Satyagraha by Gandhi
  • Successful - law was repealed
1942

Quit India Movement

Movement during WWII to force British to leave India immediately.

  • "Do or Die" slogan
  • Demand for immediate independence
  • Gandhi and Congress leaders arrested
  • Final struggle for freedom
1924-1925

Vaikom Satyagraha

Movement for temple entry rights of untouchables in Vaikom, Kerala.

  • For temple entry rights of untouchables
  • Gandhi spent 20 days in Vaikom
  • Against caste discrimination
  • Partial success achieved

Gandhi's Life Timeline

1869 - Birth

Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi was born on October 2 in Porbandar, Gujarat.

1888 - Studies in London

Went to London to study law at University College London and train as a barrister.

1893 - South Africa

Moved to South Africa to practice law and faced racial discrimination, sparking his activism.

1915 - Return to India

Returned to India and joined Indian National Congress, quickly becoming a prominent leader.

1917 - Champaran Satyagraha

Started first Satyagraha in India which was his first major success.

1930 - Salt Satyagraha

Broke salt law through Dandi March and gave new energy to freedom movement.

1942 - Quit India Movement

Started final major movement against British with "Do or Die" slogan.

1947 - Indian Independence

India gained independence from British rule under Gandhi's leadership.

1948 - Assassination

Assassinated on January 30 in New Delhi by Nathuram Godse, a Hindu nationalist.

Gandhian Principles

Non-Violence (Ahimsa)

Practice of non-violence in thought, word, and deed. Gandhi believed this was the most powerful weapon for the oppressed.

Truth (Satya)

Gandhi's lifelong pursuit of truth. He believed that truth is God and one must always speak and act truthfully.

Self-Reliance (Swadeshi)

Emphasis on using locally made goods and being self-sufficient to empower local economies and communities.
